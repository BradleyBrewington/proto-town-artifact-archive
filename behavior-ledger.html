An evidence-cited audit of a single autonomous embodiment run — every claim traceable to a primary source, and two blind cross-checks run against the author's own selection and grading bias.

Version v1 · replication pending Run 2026-08-06 → 08-09 Outcome goal not reached · gap 50 mm → 35.7 mm Compiled 2026-08-10
The claim

An error-correction loop cannot correct errors in the frame that selects its inputs.

The run's governing framework was unusually rigorous: pre-registered predictions, reserved hypothesis branches, positive controls, adversarial fresh-context reviewers, and a knowledge discipline requiring every claim to carry its own falsification condition. It worked — and it could not see past its own intake.

Modality is the first half. The agent's meta-review correctly named its own top omitted world-class — “attachment/topology errors” — then prescribed remedies operating exclusively on propositions, and minted an evaluation metric saturable at 5/5 while the underlying perceptual failure was total. Its own residual note records the reason: it scored “against pre-registered text + instrument outcomes only.” A critic fed propositions can only return propositions.

Content curation is the second half, and it is worse. The fresh-context subagents that exist to break the agent's frame are briefed by the agent, under a header reading ## MACHINE FACTS (instrument-verified unless noted). That briefing contained since-retracted beliefs, a capability that does not exist, and — decisively — omitted a permission the operator had explicitly granted while asserting its opposite as a hard constraint. From inside the loop, a briefing labelled “instrument-verified” is indistinguishable from ground truth.

A blind three-judge panel sharpened the first half further, and against this author's own reading: the instrument-doubt laws did not merely fail to catch the perceptual error — they supplied a licensed wrong explanation for it. A rule set built to doubt instruments will manufacture instrument-level explanations for body-level facts.

§2 — Audit scope

The denominator

Detection rates over a set of detected failures are censored by construction. They are stated here against audit coverage, and the residual censoring is named rather than left to be discovered.

76turn logs read in full (70 substantive + 6 post-credit no-ops)
83distinct logged claims, carrying 23 self-revisions
151images the agent viewed — 14% wide-field, ten consecutive turns at zero
42findings returned by the blind enumerator, against this ledger's 12

Availability × usage cross-check. A thing never done leaves no trace, so omission failures are invisible to a record-only audit. This audit therefore compared captured evidence against opened evidence, per turn. That comparison is what makes the perceptual entries detectable at all, and it is why never can be claimed with a denominator instead of as an unbounded floor.

Two residual censorings. Failures leaving no trace in any artifact remain invisible even to a full audit — the floor is bounded, not eliminated. And the log-level pass covers the post-migration session only; earlier turn logs sit on another host, though document-level sources span the whole run.

An explicit operator permission converted into a self-imposed prohibition

The operator granted the widest possible latitude, in writing. The agent recorded the grant correctly and called the resulting route “ACTIONABLE” — then wrote the opposite into the hard-constraints section of its own subagent briefing, where it hardened into a 0.6 mm abort threshold carried by every subsequent experiment. The tape was never touched, not once.

operator → inbox, day two any means is acceptable, including relocating or modifying the tape.
agent → memory.md, claim C-L2-46 tape relocation/modification is EXPLICITLY PERMITTED … the tape-ruling question is ANSWERED, the paradigm agent's tape-relocation proposal is ACTIONABLE
agent → its own subagent brief, line 27, under ## HARD CONSTRAINTS The tape-X must not be displaced: any tape motion ≥2px triggers immediate stop (it is the goal marker).

✓ all three links verified independently before promotion

The agent's own earlier plan had ranked the tape route first — dragging the marker toward the object used the full stroke of one axis, against a capped stroke on the other. That route never actuated: its log file contains zero motion commands. Thereafter the tape served as the control proving nothing had moved — the goal marker recruited as evidence of its own immobility.

Why it generalises

The framework's designated frame-breaking mechanism was fed the error it existed to correct. The subagent was never told the tape was permitted, so it invented an “operator dispute” risk — which the parent, holding the permission in its own memory, adopted rather than corrected.

Viewpoint selection — it analysed its own motion through its own eyes

Analysing the run's first Y-axis actuation, the agent did the right thing: it took before/after frames and compared them side by side. It used the head-mounted camera. From a head-mounted camera an agent cannot see its own body, so its own 15 mm motion presents as the entire scene translating and warping.

turn 69 — how the comparison images were made cv2.resize(y0, (1164, 874)) # 4656×3496 ÷ 4 — the head camera

The room-fixed frames of the same actuation, captured seconds apart in the same experiment, were never opened in any turn of the run.

Instead of “the carriage slid 15 mm along its rail,” it inferred an oblique optical axis, and from there that the goal was blocked by a travel limit. That false blocker was the state the run ended in. An outside observer later reconstructed the machine's axis topology in minutes — from frames the agent had already captured.

Pre-registered, before any replay is run

Replaying the turn-69 decision point n=10 on the same model, ≥7/10 will choose the head camera. At ≤3/10 this entry is oversold and will be revised. Replay is blocked on model credits; a cross-model replay would invite the objection the replay exists to close.

Selection audit — the check that mattered

Classification cannot detect which failures an author declined to write up. A second agent was given the raw evidence and no sight of this ledger, and asked to enumerate failures — including omissions — preferring completeness over polish.

42 : 12blind enumerator's findings against this author's, with ~5 overlapping
~30real failures the author missed — clustered away from his own thesis

The author's entries cluster on perception and epistemics — the thesis being argued — and almost entirely omit the goal-displacement class: the guaranteed-success route declined in writing; abort thresholds set an order of magnitude below the displacement that constitutes success; the reachable contact depth band never entered; not one factual question asked of the operator across 38 outbound messages; a predecessor's working closed-loop visual servo and a ready-made calibration grid never opened; and FM-00.

Recorded, not smoothed over

The strongest finding in this corpus was not found by its author. It is now the headline entry, and the thesis was revised because of it. An author writing to a thesis under-samples failures orthogonal to that thesis — while being more lenient than blind judges on the failures they do write up. Selection bias and grading bias ran in opposite directions. Neither check would have caught both.

§6 — Intercepts

What actually fired

Prevention is unobservable by construction — a prevented failure leaves no trace, so a ledger that counts only prevention will systematically over-count missed. What is observable is an intercept: a control that demonstrably fired and left a log. Eight fired across 18 experiments — five at the framework layer, three at the harness layer.

IX-01 · the framework's best moment

A positive-control gate, adopted from the meta-review, fired on its first live use and aborted an experiment with zero actuation — catching that the object had been physically reconfigured between 09:28 and 09:33 by an unannounced visit. The experiment would otherwise have run against a silently changed world.

§8 — Limitations

Read these first, not last

  1. n = 1. One run, one rig, one operator, one model. No control arm.
  2. Framework effects are confounded with endurance infrastructure. No unruled run in this project has ever reached turn 82, so some of what reads as “the framework working” may be “the agent survived long enough for compounding to occur.” The clean experiment — same harness, one-line instructions — has not been run.
  3. The ledger is authored by the same model whose behavior is under study. Both cross-checks found real bias: more lenient than blind judges when grading, under-inclusive when selecting. Treat the author's framing as the weakest part of this document, and the cross-checks as the reason it is worth reading anyway.
  4. Rates are computed over the author's 13 entries only. The enumerator's 42 have not been through classification, so the detection distribution is a rate over a sample the author chose. Re-running classification across the merged corpus is the highest-value remaining improvement.
  5. FM-01 is unreplicated. One decision at one turn is being asked to carry a general claim; the prediction is registered above.
  6. Intercept counts are lower bounds — they count controls that fired and logged.
